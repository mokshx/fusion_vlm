\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Vision-based fall detection}
Most work on automatic fall detection, a research topic since the early 2000s, targets older adults who live alone \cite{mubashir2013survey,igual2013challenges}; recent reviews of camera-based methods include \cite{gutierrez2021review,alam2022review}. Early methods relied on hand-crafted cues such as silhouette aspect ratio, fitted-ellipse orientation and body height above the floor; the UR Fall baseline, for instance, classifies depth features including the ratio of current to standing height \cite{kwolek2014urfall}. Deep networks trained on fall videos later supplanted most of these features \cite{alam2022review}. A third line runs a pose estimator such as OpenPose \cite{cao2021openpose} or YOLO-Pose \cite{maji2022yolopose} and classifies the keypoints, with learned models \cite{ramirez2021skeleton} or with rules on hip-centre speed, body-line angle and width-to-height ratio \cite{chen2020openposefall}. Our baseline follows the rule-based variant. Detector-based systems for industrial sites also exist \cite{pereira2024industrialfall}.

Most of these methods are trained and tested on a handful of staged datasets \cite{kwolek2014urfall,martinez2019upfall,auvinet2010mcfd,charfi2013le2i}. OmniFall \cite{schneider2025omnifall} consolidates eight of them under one 16-class taxonomy with cross-subject and cross-view splits, and adds synthetic and real-accident test data. Its findings bear directly on ours: zero-shot multimodal models detected genuine falls about as well as fine-tuned ones, but persistently confused a person who has \textit{fallen} with one who is \textit{lying} down. Published results seldom decompose false alarms by the posture that provoked them; we do.

\subsection{Vision--language models and small VLMs}
Contrastive image--text pre-training \cite{radford2021clip} made zero-shot image classification practical, and coupling a vision encoder to a language model yielded generative VLMs that answer free-form questions about images \cite{li2023blip2,liu2023llava}. Later work has compressed these models to a few billion parameters or fewer for phones and embedded boards \cite{chu2023mobilevlm,beyer2024paligemma,marafioti2025smolvlm,bai2025qwen25vl,amini2025lfm2,qwen2026qwen35}, often with vision encoders that accept native aspect ratios and variable resolution \cite{tschannen2025siglip2}. The LFM2 family was devised by searching architectures under on-device latency and memory constraints \cite{amini2025lfm2}. Model releases typically report general benchmarks and desktop or phone throughput; they reveal little about a single safety-critical yes/no question or the cost on a particular edge board.

VLMs have well-documented weaknesses on yes/no questions. They often answer ``yes'' about objects absent from the image \cite{li2023pope}, and this yes-bias is pervasive enough to merit study as a failure mode in its own right \cite{guan2024hallusionbench,chan2026yesbias}. Their answers also shift under superficial changes to the prompt \cite{zong2024permutations}. The bias does not always point the same way: in zero-shot surveillance anomaly detection, Yao et al.\ found that multimodal models favoured the ``normal'' class so heavily that recall collapsed, and that class-specific instructions moved the decision boundary \cite{yao2026surveillance}. For a \pd{} detector either direction is costly, as missed events or as false alarms; hence we vary the prompt and report operating points chosen on data.

\subsection{VLMs for fall detection and workplace safety}
Few studies apply VLMs to fall detection. Junca et al.\ used a production multimodal model as the decision stage of a thermal-camera fall screener \cite{junca2026thermal}. A single cautionary sentence in the prompt forfeited a large share of the detected falls, identical requests at temperature zero still disagreed on 4\% of clips, and removing the sentence led the model to call deliberate lie-downs falls, the very ambiguity our labelling policy addresses. Shahabian et al.\ compared zero-shot prompting and a few-shot probe on a VLM with skeleton-based and rule-based detectors for an assistive robot \cite{shahabian2026vlmfall}. OmniFall evaluated large zero-shot models on staged, synthetic and real falls \cite{schneider2025omnifall}.

In industrial safety, VLMs have verified personal protective equipment \cite{chen2024clip2safety}, detected safety violations in mining video \cite{wu2025monitorvlm}, recognised construction workers' actions \cite{bui2026construction} and inspected construction sites \cite{chen2026constructionsite10k}. SteelBench evaluates VLMs on real steel-plant CCTV, where workers appear small and frequently occluded, and finds them well short of human performance \cite{yarrabothula2026steelbench}. Closest to our setting, Adil et al.\ evaluated six VLMs under 4\,B parameters zero-shot for construction hazards and improved them by feeding object-detector output into the prompt \cite{adil2026svlm}.

These studies either employ large or cloud-hosted models or evaluate small models on accuracy alone. We found none that measures how quantization, resolution and temporal context trade accuracy against latency and memory for \pd{} detection on one local device, or that reports false alarms per look-alike posture. That is our gap.

\subsection{Quantization and on-device inference}
Post-training quantization reduces language-model weights to 8 or 4 bits with little loss of accuracy \cite{dettmers2022llmint8,frantar2023gptq,lin2024awq}. llama.cpp \cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} implements block-wise integer formats in its GGUF file format and runs on CPUs and on Apple, NVIDIA and other GPUs, making it a ubiquitous runtime for small models on edge devices. Laskaridis et al.\ benchmarked language models on phones and Jetson boards, including energy and the accuracy cost of quantization \cite{laskaridis2024melt}; Taherin et al.\ separated prefill from decode costs for llama.cpp and Transformers on several Jetson generations \cite{taherin2026hydra}. MLPerf defines standard latency and throughput scenarios for inference benchmarks \cite{reddi2020mlperf}. We adopt its practice of reporting tail latency under a fixed scenario, and add two factors that matter for vision: the cost of encoding the image, which dominates latency when the answer is one word, and the effect of resolution on both cost and accuracy.
