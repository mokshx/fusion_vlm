\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Pilot study}
\label{sec:pilot}
Before the full grid, we tested the pipeline on 30 UR Fall frames: 10 \pd{} (five side-camera, five ceiling-camera), 10 hard negatives (crouching, kneeling, bending, sitting on a chair or bed, lying on a bed or sofa) and 10 normal, one frame per sequence, each reviewed visually. We ran LFM2.5-VL-450M, LFM2.5-VL-3B and Qwen3.5-2B at Q8\_0 with all three prompts. The pilot is too small to support conclusions about the models: the 3\,B model's hard-negative false-alarm rate with prompt~\promptA{}, 2/10, has a 95\% bootstrap interval of \PilotLargeAFaHardCI, and a perfect 10/10 recall has a 95\% Wilson interval of \PilotRecallWilsonCI. We report it because it shaped the protocol and the prompt set.

Four observations carried into the design. First, output format was a non-issue: all \PilotNAnswers{} answers across models, prompts and backends began with ``yes'' or ``no'' (\PilotNLenient{} needed lenient parsing, \PilotNUnparsed{} were unparsed), so constrained decoding was unnecessary. Second, prompt wording moved the false-alarm rate more than model size did (Table~\ref{tab:pilot}). The detailed definition in prompt~\promptB{} raised the 3\,B model's hard-negative false alarms from \PilotLargeAFaHard{} to \PilotLargeBFaHard{} and made the 450\,M model answer ``yes'' to \PilotSmallBFaNormal{} frames of people merely standing or walking; listing the excluded postures appears to prime the model toward them. This prompted \promptBtwo{}, which defines \pd{} by one physical criterion and names no excluded posture. Its effect depended on the model: it eliminated every hard-negative false alarm for Qwen3.5-2B (\PilotQwenBtwoFaHard{}, against \PilotQwenAFaHard{} with \promptA), restored the 3\,B LFM model to \PilotLargeBtwoFaHard{}, and did not help the 450\,M model (\PilotSmallBtwoFaNormal{} normal frames flagged), whose $p_\text{yes}$ on standing people stayed just above 0.5. No prompt was best for all three. Third, with prompt~\promptA{} all three models ranked every \pd{} frame above every negative (AUROC \PilotLargeAAuroc{}), so their errors stemmed from the placement of the default threshold, not from any inability to separate the classes; hence the calibrated operating point. Fourth, answers were identical across backends: \PilotCpuGpuAgree{} matched between CPU and Metal, and \PilotQeightBfAgree{} between Q8\_0 GGUF and bf16 Transformers for the 450\,M model, the lone discrepancy being a frame with $p_\text{yes}$ near 0.5.

The labelling check proved instructive. UR Fall's depth-based annotation marks ``lying on the ground'' from the person's height above the floor, so the ``lying'' frames in its daily-living sequences conflate lying on a bed or sofa with deliberate lying on the floor. Under our policy the former are hard negatives, the latter \pd{}. All three models flagged all nine deliberate floor-lying frames under every prompt (\PilotFloorHits{} answers). Those frames were filmed in rooms other than the one where every UR Fall fall occurs, and with prompt~\promptA{} the same models rejected the standing people filmed there (\PilotSameRoomNormalRejectA{} answers), early evidence that the models respond to posture, not merely to the fall room.

On an \PilotCpu{} laptop, used solely to validate the measurement pipeline, a decision took a median of \PilotSmallCpuMedianMs\,ms (LFM 450\,M), \PilotLargeCpuMedianMs\,ms (LFM 3\,B) and \PilotQwenCpuMedianMs\,ms (Qwen 2\,B) on 10 CPU threads, and \PilotSmallGpuMedianMs, \PilotLargeGpuMedianMs{} and \PilotQwenGpuMedianMs\,ms on the integrated GPU (Table~\ref{tab:pilot_eff}). Qwen3.5-2B encodes the same frame into more tokens than the LFM models, offsetting its smaller language model. On the CPU, prefill (vision encoding and prompt processing) took \PilotSmallCpuPrefillPct--\PilotLargeCpuPrefillPct\% of the time, whereas decoding the one-word answer took a median of \PilotSmallCpuDecodeMs\,ms (450\,M) and \PilotLargeCpuDecodeMs\,ms (3\,B); input resolution should therefore be the principal lever on latency.

\subsection{Main grid: overview}
The grid comprises \RNConfigs{} VLM configurations and \RNDecisions{} model decisions on the development and test sets; \RNUnparsed{} answers were unparseable. Figures below refer to the test set (\BenchNTest{} samples) unless stated, with 95\% confidence intervals in brackets. Unless a table varies them, configurations use Q8\_0 weights, native resolution, one frame, prompt~\promptA{} and the integrated GPU. Re-running \RRetimeConfigs{} configurations to re-measure latency reproduced \RRetimeSame{} of \RRetimeTotal{} answers exactly.

\subsection{RQ1: accuracy versus prompt, model size, precision, resolution and frames}
\textit{Prompt.} Table~\ref{tab:prompts} compares the prompts at the default operating point. The plain question \promptA{} was best or tied for every model, on development-set AUROC (our criterion for the rest of the grid) and on test F1. The detailed definition \promptB{} drove LFM2.5-VL-450M to answer ``yes'' to nearly every frame, including people standing or walking, and raised false alarms for the larger LFM models. The one-sentence criterion \promptBtwo{} had the opposite effect on Qwen3.5-2B: it rendered the model conservative, and it missed about half of the falls.

\textit{Default versus calibrated operating point.} With their own yes/no answers, all five models catch at least \RQwenSDefRecall\% of falls but raise false alarms on \RQwenLDefFarHard\% (Qwen3.5-4B) to \RLfmSDefFarHard\% (LFM2.5-VL-450M) of hard negatives (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Thresholds do not transfer perfectly: on the test set, LFM2.5-VL-1.6B and LFM2.5-VL-450M caught \RLfmMCalRecall\% and \RLfmSCalRecall\% of falls rather than the targeted 95\%. After calibration, LFM2.5-VL-3B is the most accurate model (F1 \RLfmLCalFone, recall \RLfmLCalRecall\%, hard-negative FAR \RLfmLCalFarHard\%).

\textit{Weight precision.} At fixed resolution, moving from 16-bit to Q8\_0 to Q4\_K\_M altered F1 by at most 0.02 for every model at native and 512\,px input, and by at most 0.03 at 256\,px (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). LFM2.5-VL-3B at native resolution attains F1 \CLfmLHalfNatOneFone, \CLfmLQeightNatOneFone{} and \CLfmLQfourNatOneFone{} at the three precisions. For these models, 4-bit weights are a safe choice.

\textit{Input resolution.} Reducing the longest side to 512\,px cost at most 0.05 F1, lowered or preserved hard-negative false alarms for every model and precision, and cut latency by 20--40\% (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}). At 256\,px most models collapsed: F1 fell to \CLfmSQeightTwofiftysixOneFone{} for LFM2.5-VL-450M, \CLfmLQeightTwofiftysixOneFone{} for LFM2.5-VL-3B and \CQwenSQeightTwofiftysixOneFone{} for Qwen3.5-2B, chiefly through false alarms on hard negatives (\CLfmSQeightTwofiftysixOneFarHard\% for the 450\,M model). LFM2.5-VL-1.6B was the exception, retaining F1 \CLfmMQfourTwofiftysixOneFone{} at 256\,px with Q4\_K\_M weights. We have no explanation for this discrepancy; it is why that configuration sits on both Pareto frontiers.

\textit{Temporal context.} Supplying the four or eight preceding frames helped no model (Table~\ref{tab:frames}) and was detrimental to the small ones: LFM2.5-VL-450M fell from F1 \CLfmSQeightNatOneFone{} with one frame to \CLfmSQeightNatFourFone{} with four and \CLfmSQeightNatEightFone{} with eight, its hard-negative false alarms doubling. Larger models were flat or slightly worse (LFM2.5-VL-3B: \CLfmLQeightNatOneFone, \CLfmLQeightNatFourFone, \CLfmLQeightNatEightFone; Qwen3.5-4B: \CQwenLQeightNatOneFone{} throughout), while latency grew roughly in proportion to frame count, reaching \CLfmLQeightNatEightPtf\,ms for LFM2.5-VL-3B with eight frames. Zero-shot, these models do not exploit short clips; temporal reasoning must be added outside the model, for example by requiring several consecutive positive decisions.

\textit{Is a VLM needed?} The pose-and-rule baseline was far weaker than any VLM (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). YOLO11n-pose with a single frame reached F1 \CPoseNPoseNatOneFone{} and recall \CPoseNPoseNatOneDefRecall\%; clips raised F1 to \CPoseNPoseNatFourFone. No rule setting reached 95\% recall even on the development set. The culprit is the detector, not the rule: YOLO11n-pose found a person in only \RPoseDetDown\% of the \pd{} frames from the side and elevated cameras, against \RPoseDetNormal\% of frames of people standing or walking. A person lying on the floor, often at the edge of a wide-angle view, is what pose models handle worst. The baseline is fast (\RPoseNMs\,ms per frame on the GPU), so it could serve as an inexpensive first stage, but not as the detector.

\subsection{RQ2: latency, memory and energy}
\label{sec:rq2}
On the integrated GPU, p95 latency of a single-frame decision ranged from \CLfmSQfourTwofiftysixOnePtf\,ms (LFM2.5-VL-450M, Q4\_K\_M, 256\,px) to \CQwenLQeightNatOnePtf\,ms (Qwen3.5-4B, Q8\_0, native). Latency tracks image tokens, not weight size: 256\,px input cut latency by 52--68\%, whereas 4-bit weights were no faster than 8- or 16-bit ones (Table~\ref{tab:grid}), because encoding the image, which is compute-bound, dominates the cost of a one-word answer. Precision instead sets the memory floor: LFM2.5-VL-3B needs \CLfmLHalfNatOneRss\,GiB at 16-bit, \CLfmLQeightNatOneRss\,GiB at Q8\_0 and \CLfmLQfourNatOneRss\,GiB at Q4\_K\_M, and resolution barely alters it.

Running on the CPU alone was 6 to 17 times slower, typically tenfold (Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}). At one decision per second per camera, the GPU serves \CLfmSQeightFivetwelveOneStreams{} cameras with LFM2.5-VL-450M at 512\,px (F1 \CLfmSQeightFivetwelveOneFone), \CLfmMQfourTwofiftysixOneStreams{} with LFM2.5-VL-1.6B at 256\,px (F1 \CLfmMQfourTwofiftysixOneFone) and \CLfmLQeightFivetwelveOneStreams{} with LFM2.5-VL-3B at 512\,px (F1 \CLfmLQeightFivetwelveOneFone). On the CPU alone, only LFM2.5-VL-450M keeps pace with one camera at acceptable accuracy (512\,px, p95 \CLfmSQeightFivetwelveOneCpuPtf\,ms); LFM2.5-VL-3B needs \CLfmLQeightNatOneCpuPtf\,ms per decision.

Below about 130\,ms only LFM2.5-VL-450M is available, at F1 near 0.89 with 512\,px or native input. No multi-frame configuration is Pareto-optimal, and smaller models dominate Qwen3.5-4B on both frontiers.

Look-alike postures differ markedly in difficulty (Table~\ref{tab:hardneg}).
