\begin{abstract}
A worker lying on the floor after a fall or collapse needs help within minutes, and camera-based alarms for this \pd{} event should run on modest hardware near the cameras. Small vision--language models (VLMs) can answer ``is a person down?'' without task-specific training, but their limits in size, quantization and resolution are unclear. We evaluate five open VLMs of 0.45--4.7\,B parameters across three weight precisions, three input resolutions, one to eight frames per decision and three prompts, on \BenchNKept{} visually reviewed frames from two public fall datasets that separate fallen people from look-alike postures, against a pose-estimation baseline. We measure latency, memory and energy on the CPU and integrated GPU of an Apple M4 Pro. With a threshold set on held-out data, a 1.6\,B model with 4-bit weights and 256\,px input reaches F1 \CLfmMQfourTwofiftysixOneFone{} at \CLfmMQfourTwofiftysixOnePtf\,ms per decision, within \RKneeGap{} of the best configuration; on the CPU alone, only the 0.45\,B model keeps pace with one camera. Four-bit weights conserve memory rather than time, short clips and detailed prompts are detrimental, and the pose baseline overlooks many fallen people. We release labels, code and per-run results.
\end{abstract}
